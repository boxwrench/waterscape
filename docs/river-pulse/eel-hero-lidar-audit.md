# Eel Scotia Bluffs hero-camera LiDAR audit

## Finding

The current Eel terrain is a 384 × 384 DEM sampled at 14.0529679 m spacing in EPSG:32610 with NAVD88 elevations. The builder exports the whole configured bbox at the requested 384 × 384 size with bilinear interpolation from the USGS 3DEP ImageServer (`pipeline/build_river_terrain.py`, `pipeline/dem.py`). This 14.05 m grid spacing is the delivered sampling interval; the local bundle does not record the native source project, acquisition date, or input resolution. Do not describe this existing surface as native 1 m LiDAR or infer native accuracy from its 5 cm integer encoding. The 3DEP service can resample other source inputs; USGS explicitly separates 1 m DEM coverage from seamless DEM coverage and provides a dedicated 1 m availability viewer ([USGS coverage FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-coverage-3d-elevation-program-3dep-dems), [TNM Access API FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/there-api-accessing-national-map-data)).

The official TNM 1 m products API returned one 3DEP tile for each of the three 1 km target bboxes. Bounded HTTP range reads from `USGS_1m_x40y449_CA_NoCAL_Wildfires_B4_2018.tif` confirm valid 1 m bare-earth elevation samples and fully valid 11 × 11 pixel neighborhoods at all three look-at centers. The exact source and sample values are recorded below. This confirms native 1 m coverage at the hero targets; the current runtime terrain remains a separately resampled 14.05 m surface.

## Available products and state-source check

**Nearest verified native-resolution candidate — CA09 Perkins.** OpenTopography records the South Fork Eel River NCALM airborne-lidar collection, PI Jonathan Perkins (UC Santa Cruz), NSF-funded, surveyed September 4, 2009, published October 23, 2012. Its raster DEM product is 1 m; point density is 9.64 points/m². Horizontal coordinates are NAD83 (CORS96) / UTM zone 10N, EPSG:26910; vertical is NAVD88 (GEOID03), EPSG:5703; units metres. The data use license is CC BY 4.0, so credit and license terms differ from public-domain 3DEP ([OpenTopography metadata](https://portal.opentopography.org/datasetMetadata?otCollectionID=OT.092012.26910.4), [raster product](https://portal.opentopography.org/raster?opentopoID=OTSDEM.092012.26910.4)). Its project boundary is the existing [catalog footprint](../../river-pulse/data/eel_river/places/scotia_bluffs/lidar-reference-footprint.geojson); its broad bounding box is not the usable footprint. Use the boundary and product tile index to select only raster tiles intersecting the target windows. Do not fetch the 371 km² survey wholesale or the point cloud for this surface task.

**California state-hosted 1 m service — no target coverage.** DWR's `CA_NoCAL_Wildfires_B5a_2018` ImageServer metadata describes a standard 1 m bare-earth 3DEP DEM, public-domain source data, UTM metres and NAVD88 metres; the service record says it was published February 14, 2020. Its stated extent is EPSG:26910 easting 569,994–680,006 m, northing 4,209,994–4,350,006 m, while the Eel look-at points are near easting 407,722–407,942 m, northing 4,484,111–4,484,361 m. A saved exact study-bbox catalog query returned zero intersecting rasters (`dwr-dem-coverage.json`). This state-hosted product is not coverage for Scotia Bluffs ([DWR service item metadata](https://gis.water.ca.gov/arcgisimg/rest/services/elevation/CA_NoCAL_Wildfires_B5a_2018/ImageServer/info/iteminfo)).

**California state partnership — exact native 1 m product negative at the hero targets.** NOAA’s 2009–2011 Coastal Conservancy product is a hydro-flattened bare-earth DEM from lidar with nominal 1 m post spacing, collected October 15, 2009–August 2, 2011. Its index lists `C405044820DEM.tif`; I fetched only this 708,448-byte tile and the 98,452-byte tile-index ZIP. TIFF tags report 3,000 × 3,000 Float32 cells at 1 m, EPSG:3717 (NAD83(NSRS2007) / UTM 10N + NAVD88), top-left tiepoint (405000 E, 4485000 N), and NoData −3.4028234663852886e38. Each hero look-at center is NoData and each 3 × 3 neighborhood is entirely NoData. The inside-tile portions of all three 1 km target windows likewise contain zero valid cells. An exact NOAA S3 object-prefix query finds no adjacent `C408044820DEM.tif`; the broad coast-wide bounds and nominal 500 m landward corridor would have been insufficient evidence by themselves. Thus this state-partnered native 1 m DEM does not cover the fixed look-at points or bounded near-camera AOIs. NOAA reports access constraints “None,” but this record does not state an explicit license; verify terms before redistribution or use ([NOAA DEM file index](https://coast.noaa.gov/htdata/raster2/elevation/California_Lidar_DEM_2009_1131/), [NOAA DEM metadata](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/49415), [source lidar metadata and corridor](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/48166), [NOAA Coastal Lidar access guide](https://www.coast.noaa.gov/digitalcoast/data/coastallidar.html)). The bounded artifacts and S3 query responses are retained under `previews/river-pulse/rp27/lidar-audit/`; no bulk raster or point-cloud data was fetched.

**Newer California state partnership / USGS source — exact 0.5 m product negative at the hero targets.** The completed 2022 Northern California project has 0.5 m bare-earth GeoTIFFs, 0.35 m nominal pulse spacing / 8 points per square metre QL1 collection, acquired in 2022, EPSG:6339 horizontal and NAVD88 GEOID18 vertical; the product carries a CC0 1.0 Public Domain Dedication. All three centers map to MGRS tile `10TDK0784`. To validate the tile key format, a nearby published `10TDK0797` raster header reports 0.5 m pixels and UTM tiepoint (407000.25 E, 4497999.75 N), matching its 1 km MGRS tile. I queried the official USGS S3 TIFF listing by the exact target key under each of the seven work-unit prefixes; all seven responses report `KeyCount=0`, `IsTruncated=false`. Work unit 5 includes Humboldt County, but the exact tile is absent, confirming county-level inclusion does not reach these hero centers. California’s state-funded North Coast program creates public-use derivatives from recent point clouds; that derivative work does not alter this tile result ([NOAA/USGS 2022 project metadata and download paths](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/78900), [USGS project report](https://prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Elevation/metadata/CA_NorthernCA_B22/USGS_CA_NorthernCA_B22_Project_Report.pdf), [North Coast Resource Partnership derivative project update](https://northcoastresourcepartnership.org/news-item/lidar-derivatives-for-california/)).

**2023 USGS North Coast Ranges — exact negative for these targets.** NOAA metadata reports 0.5 m DEM GSD for the 2023 USGS North Coast Ranges work unit 1 and the project’s geographic extent begins east of the Eel targets; its published extent and EPT bounds exclude all three target coordinates. This is not the same data as the separate 2022 Northern California project above ([NOAA project record](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/inport/item/79080), [EPT source index](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/usgs-lidar-public/CA_NorthCoastRanges_1_B23/ept.json)).

## Bounded hero crop acquired

`pipeline/build_eel_hero_terrain.py --fetch` reads the audited 64 KiB GeoTIFF header, transforms the requested scene rectangle from EPSG:32610 to the source EPSG:26910 using pyproj, and fetches only intersecting compressed source blocks with HTTP 206 ranges. The crop is pixel window column 7408, row 5365, 800 × 750 native 1 m cells (600,000 valid cells; zero NoData). Its scene grid origin is `[600.4470536, -1749.1179920]` and its requested footprint is approximately local X 600–1400 m, Z −1750 to −1000 m. The exact header and 20 block ranges, byte counts, target-cell reads, min/max/mean, and retrieval note are recorded in `previews/river-pulse/rp27/lidar-audit/eel-hero-range-manifest.json`; only about 3.9 MB of range payload was retrieved, rather than the 394,497,744-byte full tile.

The output pair is `river-pulse/data/eel_river/places/scotia_bluffs/hero-terrain.json` and `.bin.gz`. It uses the existing `river-pulse-terrain-0.1` schema consumed by `loadRiverTerrain`, preserves native 1 m cell spacing, and retains absolute NAVD88 elevations through the standard 0.05 m uint16 encoding (maximum quantization error 0.025 m). The metadata records the source tile URL, public-domain status, source CRS, vertical datum, NoData sentinel, native resolution and source pixel window. Crop selection and grid registration used pyproj 3.8 / PROJ's selected operation `Inverse of UTM zone 10N + Inverse of NAD83 to WGS 84 (1) + UTM zone 10N` (pipeline: inverse UTM on WGS84 ellipsoid, then UTM on GRS80 ellipsoid; EPSG operation accuracy metadata 4 m). Numeric coordinate differences at the requested crop corners were under 0.12 mm, which describes this calculation's coordinate output, not the operation's geodetic accuracy. No vertical conversion was applied: the source and runtime terrain both encode NAVD88 metres.

The crop is now integrated in `river-pulse/renderer/eel.js`. The hero land patch replaces covered coarse terrain triangles, blends to the prior surface across two coarse cells, and protects the existing wet edge. Runtime uses 4 m mesh spacing for primary/phone and other views, and 2 m for desktop eye/shore; camera and vegetation ground sampling use the same source-aware blend. Root's actual Form renders show sharper cliffs, benches and gullies; Study material makes those details less pronounced. This is a source-derived visual landform, not a geology interpretation, current-condition survey, bathymetry or water-level measurement. Native source resolution and the bundle's 5 cm storage increment describe different things.
