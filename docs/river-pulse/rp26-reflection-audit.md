# RP26 Eel reflection audit

`river-pulse/renderer/authored-water.js:62-72` creates one planar reflector: its target is rotated so local +Z faces world +Y and placed at `grid.focus.y`; the water mesh itself follows terrain elevations. `ReflectorBaseNode.updateBefore` reflects the camera across that plane and installs an oblique clip plane (`vendor/three/three.webgpu.js:39511-39540, 39578-39605`). It hides the water material during the offscreen pass and restores it afterward (`:39621-39655`); `bounces:false` prevents nested reflector updates (`:39375`). The viewport-aligned sample defaults to `screenUV.flipX()` (`:39103-39104`) and authored water adds `vec2(normal.x, normal.z) * 0.035` (`authored-water.js:65`). These operations are valid for the single planar mirror model, but cannot make its reflected camera/clip plane conform to Eel's warped surface.

## RP26 evidence

Phone shoreline diagnostic renders localize the white band to the reflection path: `diagnostic-no-reflection-phone-shore.jpg` removes it; `diagnostic-no-distortion-phone-shore.jpg` retains it when the 0.035 screen-UV offset is disabled; and `diagnostic-two-sided-phone-shore.jpg` retains it with the temporary two-sided probe. Their wave phases vary, so these are qualitative probes rather than matched final pairs. Thus UV distortion and front-face culling are not necessary causes. The lower-plane capture (`diagnostic-lowered-plane-phone-shore.jpg`, target lowered 4 m to 11.7 m) makes the pale gap substantially broader. This shows plane placement changes the artifact, but lowering it is not a correction and the evidence does not establish clipping as the sole cause. A prior no-glint probe also retained the band. Height-agreement fading and zero glint were rejected earlier; neither identified the mechanism.

## Bounded next step

The +4 m target-plane probe was rejected because it produced a black/clear result (likely reflector-facing-away behavior), so it does not establish a useful alternate plane. Do not tune the shared/vendor clip implementation around it: a single plane cannot represent this nonplanar water. Keep Hacienda's default plane and sampling unchanged.

Both projected-UV variants are rejected. The plane-correct `(-0.5,-0.5)` form made the water sample mostly sky when applied off-plane; the `(-0.5,+0.5)` variant appeared to remove the band but was false reassurance: `diagnostic-uv-calibration-phone-shore.jpg` shows a green UV calibration error against `screenUV.flipX()`. The `+0.5` result therefore samples the wrong orientation. Root removed the projected branch/options/imports; do not describe either as a fix.

The current candidate is an Eel-only background guard. `eel-water-spike.js:8-10` passes `shoreReflectionRange: [0.02, 2.6]`; `authored-water.js:62, 68-75` enables reflector depth only with this option, samples it at the same distorted reflection UV, and defines `geometryConfidence = 1 - smoothstep(0.95, 0.9999, RTdepth)`. It retains reflected geometry and blends toward muted `0x294c38` only where the RTT looks like background and the modeled shallows are near shore (`smoothstep(0.02,2.6,modeledDepth)^2`). This is an authored proxy guard, not a physical Fresnel law or geometry repair. Modeled depth comes from interpolated wet-mask distance times `0.08`, capped at `2.6` (`eel-water-depth.js`); thresholds are illustrative modeled-depth values, not measured bathymetry. The branch is optional: without `shoreReflectionRange`, reflector depth is disabled and the original `fresnel` weight/color path remains, so Hacienda's existing four-argument call and defaults stay unchanged.

The final before/after image set contains 12 images forming 6 matched pairs, captured with `?freeze=1` and phase-zero water in both states; the images now correspond to the current source versions rather than an earlier shader. Across the final phone and desktop shore/eye/primary views, the broad pale/white band is strongly reduced and bank reflections remain. Thin bright rim/pixel fragments persist along the water edge. A glint-zero diagnostic retained that thin rim, so glint is not its cause; the intended strength `1.8` was restored. Treat this as a bounded visual improvement, not a clean contact edge or a fix for curved reflection geometry. Compare the reviewed pairs in `previews/eel/rp26/review.html`.

With `depth:true`, `ReflectorBaseNode.getRenderTarget` attaches a sampleable `DepthTexture` (`vendor/three/three.webgpu.js:39480-39501`); default targets already have depth testing enabled, so this exposes depth for shader sampling rather than introducing depth testing itself. Without the Eel-only range option, Hacienda's reflector remains `depth:false`. `DepthTexture()` defaults to `UnsignedIntType` and `DepthFormat` (`vendor/three/three.core.js:29600-29617`), conventionally a nominal 32-bit-per-pixel depth resource. The prior target already had an implicit depth attachment; exact incremental allocation/backend storage is not measured. Treat the reported 1.30 MiB as nominal sampled-depth size, not measured incremental or resident GPU memory.

The current material remains a trial. Its Eel optical-depth input is derived from an illustrative aerial-informed wet mask, not measured depth or stage. Hacienda continues to call `createAuthoredWater(...)` with four arguments (`river-pulse/renderer/hacienda.js:265`); keep its plane, resolution scale, UV offset, sun and material defaults intact.
