California Water Boards · CalWater source

California Interagency Watershed Mapping Committee, including DWR, Forestry and Fire Protection, Fish and Game, and Water Boards, with federal partners. 1999 boundaries / 2.2.1 documentation updated 2004. Drainage and administrative context; not inundation or a legal jurisdiction map.

Terrain resolution

The bundled 384 × 384 terrain has 14.05 m cells, resampled from the USGS 3DEP mosaic. NAVD88 metre elevations are preserved without vertical exaggeration. No 1 m DEM or raw LiDAR point cloud is bundled. The 0.05 m encoding increment is not spatial resolution or survey accuracy.

2009 NCALM / UC Santa Cruz 1 m LiDAR-derived DEM covers the station area but misses the main bluff review targets. It is a partial research reference, not California state data or the terrain rendered here. Full-study 1 m coverage remains unverified.

Geography

The USGS station anchor and 3DHP river geometry share the local metre frame. California CalWater supplies the watershed context above; it does not supply the 3D terrain or water level.

Visual interpretation

Water and gravel are separated using an authored color classification of aligned USGS/USDA NAIP mosaic imagery inside the mapped river footprint. It is a visual study, not today's wetted channel. The surface follows DEM samples as a proxy; its color and moving highlights are illustrative. No measured stage, current or bathymetry is claimed.

Form mode uses plain terrain and water colors with no animated highlights; the authored water boundary remains. Aerial mode lays the source mosaic on the same terrain. This pass adds an image-informed forest silhouette and rock surface detail in Landscape study. Detailed structures remain ahead.

Bluff and forest Setting

Tree sites follow an authored dark-green aerial heuristic, excluding mapped river footprint, steep faces and camera clearances. Generic tree assets, heights and density are visual estimates, not surveyed trees or species identification. Nearby trees use reusable ez-tree branch and needle geometry; distant crowns use the reservoir’s baked color/normal tree atlas. Rock grain, weathering and bedding are authored surface cues, not measured geological detail. The terrain elevations and water geometry are unchanged.
