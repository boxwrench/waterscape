Terrain resolution

The bundled 384 × 384 terrain has 14.05 m cells, resampled from the USGS 3DEP mosaic. NAVD88 metre elevations are preserved without vertical exaggeration. A bounded 800 × 750 m crop of a native 1 m USGS bare-earth DEM is now bundled at the three hero targets. The surrounding scene retains the 14.05 m grid. The crop renders at 2 m spacing in desktop eye/shore views and 4 m in primary and phone views. A transition blends to the previous terrain and preserves the existing modeled water edge. These are source-derived landforms; no raw LiDAR point cloud is bundled. The 0.05 m encoding increment is not spatial resolution or survey accuracy.

2009 NCALM / UC Santa Cruz 1 m LiDAR-derived DEM covers the station area but misses the main bluff review targets. Verified hero land source

USGS 3DEP one-metre bare-earth DEM, Northern California Wildfires B4 2018 project, public-domain federal data. A bounded crop was extracted from the source TIFF and projected into this scene. The encoding preserves heights to within 0.025 m; that is storage precision, not survey accuracy. Tree sites retain their authored seed and horizontal positions and are grounded on the selected land surface. Visual interpretation

Water and gravel are separated using an authored color classification of aligned USGS/USDA NAIP mosaic imagery inside the mapped river footprint. It is a visual study, not today's wetted channel. The surface follows DEM samples as a proxy; its color and moving highlights are illustrative. No measured stage, current or bathymetry is claimed.

Form mode uses plain terrain and water colors with no animated highlights; the authored water boundary remains. Aerial mode lays the source mosaic on the same terrain. This pass adds an image-informed forest silhouette and rock surface detail in Landscape study. Detailed structures remain ahead.

Bluff and forest Setting

Tree sites follow an authored dark-green aerial heuristic, excluding mapped river footprint, steep faces and camera clearances. Generic tree assets, heights and density are visual estimates, not surveyed trees or species identification. Nearby trees use reusable ez-tree branch and needle geometry; distant crowns use the reservoir’s baked color/normal tree atlas. Rock grain, weathering and bedding are authored surface cues, not measured geological detail. Trees are grounded on the selected land; the water footprint remains unchanged.

Shared reservoir surface assets

The dry bluff material reuses the reservoir’s ambientCG Rock030 color and normal maps (CC0 1.0), blended across world-space projections. Their restrained texture and normal detail are an authored visual treatment, not a photograph or geology survey of Scotia Bluffs. Review tools retain the previous procedural material for comparison; Form and Source aerial exclude this treatment.

River water optics

The river water reuses Hacienda's reflections, filtered ripples and refraction onto a modeled gravel bed on the same refined Eel surface. Optical depth comes from an authored distance-to-shore treatment, not measured depth or bathymetry. Water color, clarity and motion remain illustrative. The reflection uses one horizontal plane near the Bluffs view; the DEM-following surface varies in height, so reflection alignment can be imperfect elsewhere. A visual guard mutes reflected background over modeled shallows to reduce bright bank gaps; it does not repair the surface or simulate physical shoreline optics. No gauge drives water level or current. Improved water is the default with the refined shoreline in Landscape study. Review tools retain the previous surface for comparison; Form and Source aerial preserve their separate presentations.
