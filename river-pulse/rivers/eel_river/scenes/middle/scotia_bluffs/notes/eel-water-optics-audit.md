# RP25 Eel water optics audit

## Compatibility boundary

`createAuthoredWater` already accepts a caller-supplied grid as its fourth argument. Use that seam for an Eel-only spike: supply the existing Eel clipped water surface positions and indices plus an explicitly authored `opticalDepth` per vertex and a focus. Do not route Eel through `buildAuthoredWaterGeometry`; that helper selects Russian River features by name and builds its own straight, local corridor. Keep the adapter and selectable material state local to Eel. Hacienda must keep the existing default call and appearance, and the reservoir engine, kernel and vendor files stay untouched.

Eel's existing refined water triangles are clipped from the mapped river footprint and lifted 0.18 m over sampled 3DEP terrain. They therefore follow the roughly 14 m DEM rather than a single horizontal plane. The material's refraction can use the supplied illustrative depth attribute, but the planar reflector target is fixed at `grid.focus.y`. On this warped surface the reflection can float, shear, or show a single-plane horizon mismatch, especially where water spans steep cross-channel terrain. Keep the first test confined to the existing local clipped surface and fixed shoreline/eye views. Check winding: Eel's `[a,b,c]` clipped triangles are upward-facing for x-east/z-south coordinates; retain that winding because the authored material uses `FrontSide`. Check for holes, terrain intersections, hillside draping and edge lift changes against the prior proxy.

The proposed depth field is an illustrative input; the reflected bed, refraction, caustics, clarity and glints are optical effects derived from that modeled input and authored light. The 3DHP footprint is mapped hydrography; the terrain is a 3DEP service grid. Neither supplies local water depth, surveyed bathymetry, exact present shoreline, stage, turbidity or current. Keep source/form modes, California context, terrain, forest and the existing footprint geometry available unchanged. Do not label modeled `opticalDepth` as measurement.

## Lighting and bounded safeguards

Hacienda's shader hard-codes `sun = normalize(vec3(0.68, 0.73, 0.18))`. Eel's directional light is at `(-1600, 2300, 700)`, approximately `(-0.55, 0.79, 0.24)` from the scene origin; Eel's old proxy glint also uses a negative-x sun vector. Hacienda's hard-coded positive-x sun therefore disagrees with Eel's scene light. Let Eel supply its own light direction through an optional per-call setting while preserving Hacienda's current default exactly. Do not change Eel's actual light or fixed cameras to fit the borrowed shader.

Keep the previous water proxy as the default until review. Compare the same geometry and camera in settled Bluffs, across-river eye and shoreline desktop/phone views, then overview/bend and form/source views. Inspect surface continuity, clipped edge, light direction, false bright glints, reflection mismatch and phone-scale shimmer. Confirm motion pause behavior and no console errors or overflow. Only water optics should differ in the paired view.

## First RP25 render and integration audit

The final paused comparisons are `previews/eel/rp25/before-shore.jpg` / `after-shore.jpg` and `before-eye.jpg` / `after-eye.jpg`; `after-primary.jpg` shows the reach view. The recaptured previous-water shore and eye images show plain pale blue water with no white band. The optics images show a dark green reflection that improves visual depth, but also a strong white angular band along the far-bank edge. In these matched captures the band appears only with the optics pass, which locates the regression to that pass but does not prove its mechanism. A diagnostic capture, `iteration-no-glint-shore.jpg`, retains the band with glint strength set to zero; glint is therefore not its sole cause. The y=15.70 m planar reflector is being used under a clipped surface that ranges from 12.08 to 21.20 m, making a nonplanar reflection mismatch a major unresolved issue; terrain intersections or contact geometry may also contribute and need isolation. A height-agreement fade was tested and rejected: it removed much of the forest reflection and flattened the water, yet left the white band. That failure does not diagnose the band's cause. Keep RP25 a visual spike; these captures do not justify shared-water extraction yet.

The attempted Eel-only plane-agreement fade (`[0.25, 1.5]` m, fallback `0x79938b`) was rejected after `previews/eel/rp25/iteration-height-shore.jpg`. It removed much of the forest reflection and made the water read as flat gray-green, while the white jagged far-bank edge remained. Root removed the option and branch. Current `authored-water.js` has no reflection-height fade; Hacienda's original default behavior remains intact. A separate `glintStrength: 0` diagnostic also left the band (`iteration-no-glint-shore.jpg`); the temporary option/test was reverted, and the diagnostic is negative evidence against glint as the sole cause, not proof of a reflection-plane cause.

The current bounded refinement is `filterProceduralBed: true` for Eel only. It attenuates the hash-based gravel contrast toward its mean (`0.5`) using `exp(-1.5 * fwidth(bedUV * 7).length())`, reducing subpixel cell variation in grazing views. Hacienda's ordinary call leaves the option false, so it retains the original procedural calculation; its loaded pebble map path is also separate from Eel's procedural bed. In the final `after-shore.jpg`, the bed retains some fine mottling without the initial strong gold strip, but the shoreline white band remains unresolved. This supports keeping the filter in the experimental Eel pass for review, not treating the overall water optics as accepted. In final code, reflector resolution is fixed at the original 0.65; the unused optional resolution setting has been removed. The default Hacienda call still omits the Eel-only bed filter and retains its original sun default.

`eel-water-spike.js` passes the original refined surface position and index `BufferAttribute` objects through to the optical mesh; its `BufferGeometry` wrapper adds only the optical-depth attribute (about 0.091 MiB in the reported sample). The depth mapper returns the same position and index arrays it receives, so this path does not allocate duplicate source geometry buffers. It estimates depth from distance to the 0.5 threshold in the smoothed, aerial-informed wet field, capped at 2.6 m with 0.08 m per map metre. This is a deterministic optical-depth model, not measured water depth. The reflector counters and memory limits are recorded above.

Pause behavior is structurally sound in `eel.js`: animation seconds advance only when unpaused in study mode, and the optical clock receives those frozen seconds. The adapter's `update(seconds, false)` does not itself enforce reduced motion, but the caller's frozen clock does; keep this call contract explicit if it is refactored.
